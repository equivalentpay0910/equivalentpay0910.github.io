I'm a Data Science student at San José State University (minors in Statistics + Computer Science, graduating Dec 2027), based in the SF Bay Area. I like building software people actually use, from backend services and data pipelines to AI agents, and I care most about making systems you can trust.

Right now I'm leading and building LegalBee at SJSU's Responsible Computing Club: a jurisdiction-aware RAG copilot that answers legal questions from government, court + legal-aid sources, with citations and explicit confidence signals. I'm also the team's de facto PM, setting the roadmap, running weekly sprints and keeping product, design + engineering in sync.

I'm also developing Daybeam, an AI planning agent. Ask it to plan your Saturday in San José (farmers market before noon, lunch under $20, home by 6) and it builds the whole day, checked against real travel times, opening hours, budget and time windows. It compares one-shot, ReAct and beam-search planning to answer one question: does extra search buy more correct plans, and is it worth the cost?

On the research side, I'm an ML Research Assistant on two projects: applying diffusion models (SR3) to video-rate fluorescence microscopy super-resolution, and building logistic regression, HLM and propensity score matching models on 70K+ student records to evaluate SJSU's Learning Assistant Program. We're now testing whether there's an instructor effect behind the program's success.

Other recent builds include a full-stack agent on Anthropic's Claude tool-calling API and a multimodal Trust & Safety risk pipeline.

Daybeam

Growing
Python · LLM tool-calling · one-shot vs. ReAct vs. beam search · A* route planning · Streamlit

An AI planning agent that turns "plan my Saturday in San José: farmers market before noon, lunch under $20, home by 6" into a full day plan, checked against real travel times, opening hours, budget and time windows. The point is to compare one-shot, ReAct and beam-search planning, the same trade-off behind agents like Gemini and Copilot: does extra search produce more correct plans, and is it worth the extra cost? Built on tool calls to an A* route planner over San José roads, a places lookup and a code-based checker that verifies every constraint, then evaluated on hundreds of multi-constraint test tasks for success rate, cost and speed. Streamlit app in progress.
